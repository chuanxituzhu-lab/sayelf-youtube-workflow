# YouTube Policy Guard v0.1

> This is a preflight checklist, not legal advice and not a substitute for the current YouTube Studio decision. Checked 2026-09-14.

## Guard order

```text
Content + title + thumbnail + description + tags + audio/visual assets
  → Community Guidelines
  → rights / copyright
  → monetization and advertiser suitability
  → reused / inauthentic content
  → altered or synthetic disclosure
  → human decision when uncertain
```

## Policy checks

### 1. Community Guidelines

Check the full package, including thumbnails, links, comments or other attached elements when in scope. Look for harmful or dangerous content, harassment, hate, sexual content, child safety risks, graphic violence, regulated goods, spam/deceptive practices, and misinformation categories that apply to the topic. Context can matter, but “educational” is not a blanket exemption.

Decision: `PASS` if no material concern is found; `WARN` if context or editing needs review; `BLOCK` when a likely violation cannot be corrected before publishing.

Source: [YouTube’s Community Guidelines](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9288567?hl=en)

### 2. Rights and copyright

For every external visual, recording, music track, voice, quote, or likeness, record the source and the permission/license basis. Do not assume “found online”, attribution, a short clip, or fair use automatically clears a claim. Where rights or jurisdiction are unclear, stop for human/legal review.

Source: [Copyright on YouTube](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2797466?hl=en)

### 3. Monetization and advertiser suitability

Check original value, context, title, thumbnail, description, tags, and the full video. Advertiser suitability can differ from whether content is allowed on YouTube. Mark potential limited/no ad suitability as `WARN` and let the creator choose; a Community Guidelines or rights issue remains `BLOCK`.

Source: [Advertiser-friendly content guidelines](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278?hl=en)

### 4. Reused and inauthentic content

Do not design a channel around mass-produced, repetitive, or templated videos with little original value. If using third-party material, identify the creator’s meaningful commentary, transformation, education, entertainment, or original perspective. This policy is separate from copyright: permission alone does not guarantee monetization.

Source: [YouTube Channel Monetisation Policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en-GB)

### 5. Altered or synthetic content

Ask whether a realistic person, event, place, or scene has been meaningfully altered or generated. If yes, require the creator to review YouTube Studio’s altered-content disclosure setting before publishing. This skill does not decide whether a disclosure is legally or factually sufficient; it records the required review.

Source: [Disclosing use of altered or synthetic content](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en)

## Stop conditions

Stop and ask the creator to resolve or review when:

- ownership, permission, license scope, or likeness consent is unknown;
- a realistic altered/synthetic depiction may require disclosure;
- the package relies on misleading expectations or prohibited deception;
- the content may violate Community Guidelines or involves a high-risk sensitive topic;
- the creator asks to publish without an explicit final review.

Before a sensitive publish, re-open the official source pages. Policy text and enforcement can change; old checks remain historical evidence and do not silently become current approval.

For recent policy changes, also review [Upcoming and recent ad guideline updates](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9725604?hl=en) before relying on an older monetization conclusion.
